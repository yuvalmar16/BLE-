What the method actually is

1. Cross-sensor identity matching

Optimal bipartite assignment (Hungarian algorithm) between Sensor-A and Sensor-B travelers within each time window, on a score combining:

  • Transit-time plausibility — a bounded Gaussian in the transit gap between a traveler's boundary sessions (peak ≈10s, spread 60s, gated to a max 180s transit / 60s coverage-overlap allowance)
  • Boundary-order agreement (order_weight=0.25) — do the first-seen/last-seen orderings across both sensors agree with the candidate direction
  • Neighborhood-consistency (nc_weight=2.0, 1 neighbor each side) — a bonus for how well a traveler's nearest temporal neighbors' own best candidates agree in relative order. The strongest single addition found: improves held-out loss, share error, and correlation simultaneously at zero matched-volume cost, and is the only new scoring term in this project's history chosen identically by every leave-one-date-out fold rather than overfitting one unstable date.
  • Same-MAC bonus (mac_bonus=3.0, binary equality) — a bonus when the two candidate travelers share a raw MAC address across sensors. Rare (~4% of matches) since MAC randomization defeats it for most travelers, but the subset that does share a MAC is measurably cleaner (more often mutual-best, faster and more physically plausible transit times) and improves the metrics further at negligible cost. Verified non-circular: Stage C's per-sensor clustering never uses raw MAC identity or cross-sensor information, so this is genuinely new evidence.
  • RSSI shape (rssi_weight=0.0) — tested extensively (whole-session trend, local slope, peak timing, full resampled trajectory shape); none of it carries usable directional signal in this dataset, so it's calibrated off.

2. Direction labeling

Simplified to the trivial rule, confirmed equivalent to the full scoring-based choice on 100% of matched pairs (626/626) before being adopted:

  • last_seen_A < last_seen_B → A→B
  • last_seen_B < last_seen_A → B→A
  • exact tie → first_seen breaks it; if that's also tied, ambiguous

The complexity in the matching score earns its keep entirely in deciding which pair wins; it contributes nothing beyond this rule to the direction label of whichever pair does win.

Two consecutive, independently-validated improvements, each confirmed on real held-out data rather than assumed. RSSI features, hard/soft order-preservation constraints, aggregate flow estimation, candidate pruning, mutual-best-only matching, and anchor-window matching were all tested separately and did not beat this configuration — see the design doc for the full negative-result record.

Known limitation — two structurally unreachable dates. 2026-05-19 and 2026-05-31 have a Stage-C traveler count below the manual total no matter how Stage D scores candidates — matching cannot recover volume that was never resolved as distinct travelers upstream. Diagnosed in a dedicated follow-up: 2026-05-31 is a raw BLE-sensing ceiling (Sensor B detected only 40 raw MAC addresses against a manual count of 56 — unfixable at any pipeline stage). 2026-05-19 is genuine Stage C over-merging (crowded periods produce large clusters of temporally-overlapping, similar-RSSI-trend sessions from different people). A candidate fix (BLOCK_GAP_THRESHOLD_S: 60→30s) is validated and recommended but not yet incorporated into this method — held-out loss 901.0→881.6 if adopted, verified non-pathological across the full 30-day dataset, but it trades some accuracy on 2026-05-09 for improvement on 2026-05-19, so it's staged as a separate decision.
Statistical power. All comparisons in this project are over 8 ground-truth dates. Every metric above should be read as "the best-supported estimate given the data," not as a guarantee — correlation in particular swings substantially between similar-looking configurations at this sample size.
